Seziz Gwengamp (fr)

The siege of Guingamp

Ton

1. Gatekeeper, you must open this door!
It's Lord Rohan who stays before,
With him twelve thousand soldiers have come
To lay siege to Guingamp town!

2. To you this gate shall never open be
To you or to anybody,
Without our Duchess Anna's leave,
Since of this town command she received.

3. - Duchess, shall we open this gate
To the felon prince who's in wait
With the twelve thousand soldiers of his camp,
That have come to lay siege to Guingamp?

4. - All my doors are safe, shut with lock and key,
All my walls as strong as can be.
To obey them, I'm sure, would be a shame.
Guingamp is a town they shall never tame.

5. They may besiege us for whole eighteen months,
But enter Guingamp town they won't;
Just load your guns, and toil and strain accept!
Let's see who shall most his attempt regret!

6.- There are here thirty cannonballs in store,
Yes, thirty gunballs, if not more;
Neither gunpowder shall run short
Nor lead to be cast, nor tin to be poured.

7. On his way back as he climbed up the tower,
From a gunshot wound was injured
A bullet that had been shot from the camp
By a man known by the name Gwazgaram. 		8. The duchess Anne who would not cease from strife,
Has said to the brave gunner's wife:
- My God, my God, what shall we do?
Now that your poor husband is wounded, too!

9. - Even if my poor husband were dead,
I could stand in for him, instead!
His gun being served by no other than me,
Thunder and fire, and we should see! -

10. But as they all to resist themselves braced,
The town walls fell down at one place,
To battering rams the gates gave way,
And the town, full of soldiers, was at bay.

11. - Soldiers, for you the pretty girls you hold
For me the silver and the gold,
And all the treasures that in Guingamp hide,
And the town itself I put on my side! -

12. The Duchess, hearing the talk of the town,
On her knees has thrown herself down:
- Lady of Bon-Secours, I pray to you,
Please, rescue us, as you usually do! -

13. The Duchess Anna, on hearing so much,
Ran hastily towards the church,
And she threw herself down upon her knees
On the altar's cold and bare stone degrees:

14. - Do you want, Virgin Mary, - could it be -,
Your house changed to stable to see,
Your sacristy used as a vault for wine,
Your high altar as table where to dine - 		15. She was with her prayer still going on,
When panic broke out in the town,
'Cause of a gun that had been fired
And nine hundred people who had died.

16. It was indeed a most terrible shake;
The houses were all made to quake,
While bells were heard ringing in a high tone,
Ringing by themselves, everywhere in town.

17. - Page, little page, I wish that you might,
As you are nimble, quick and light,
Run fast to the top of the flat-roofed tower,
See who sets the bells swinging, at this hour.

18. A good sword is hanging on your side.
If anyone up there you find,
Anyone who's swinging the bell,
Stab him through the heart, and you shall do well! -

19. When the page went up, he was singing;
When he went down, he was trembling.
- Up the flat-roofed tower I have gone,
Upstairs when I came, I have seen no one;

20. Upstairs I saw no one, no one, barring
The blessed Most Holy Virgin,
The Virgin, with her holy Son, that's true
Who have set the bells swinging as they do. -

21. The felon prince to his soldiers has said,
When this report to him was made:
- Let's saddle horses and yield to retraint!
For we must leave their houses to the saints! -


In 1486 Guingamp was besieged by the par French under Viscount of Rohan's command.
The defender, Roland Gouyquet, is wounded and the town invested by treason.
As soon as he is healed, he comes back and the invaders leave the town.
According to the present song, this turn of fortune is due to the presence
of the Duchess Anne (which is not historically documented) and to the intercession
of Our Lady of Bon Secours.
